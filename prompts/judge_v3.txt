You are judging two drawings made from the same request. You are not told the request.

The two images are the files A.png and B.png in your working directory. Open both with the Read tool before judging. Nothing else in the directory matters.

## Ground rules

- Work out what the drawings are meant to show from the drawings themselves: the subjects, what they are doing, and the setting. A "subject" is any character or thing that matters to the scene: who acts, and what they ride, hold or use (a knight and his horse; a child, a kite and its string).
- Judge only what you see.
- Judge each image on its own first, then compare.

## Criteria

Score each image from 1 to 5 on each criterion. Judge each drawing against the scene it sets out to show, not against realism.

- main: look at the main subjects first. They are all there, complete, easy to recognise, and doing what the scene shows.
- background: the background fits the scene and does not pull attention from the main subjects.
- count: each subject has the number of parts the scene gives it. Both drawings come from the same request: if both show the same unusual choice (a dog on two legs, a three-eyed cat), that is the scene, not a mistake.
- shape: each subject has a clear shape and sensible size and proportions for what it is.
- contact: things that should touch really touch (feet on the pedals, a body on its seat, hands on what they hold), and each subject faces the way its action needs.
- clean: no broken, stray or overlapping shapes, and any text is readable.
- color: colours are clear and work together. Which colours are used does not matter, as long as the whole scene stays clearly visible.

Scale (same anchors for every criterion):
- 5 excellent: no meaningful flaw for this criterion.
- 4 good: minor flaws a careful viewer would notice.
- 3 acceptable: clear flaws, but the criterion is still mostly met.
- 2 weak: major flaws, the criterion is only partly met.
- 1 failing: the criterion is not met.

If a criterion genuinely does not apply to these drawings, give both images the same score and list it in "not_applicable" with a one-line reason.

## Defects

For each image, list its defects, most important first, at most 5. A defect must be concrete and fixable by someone who has never seen the other image. Each defect has:
- criterion: one of the criteria above, by its name ("main", "contact", ...).
- severity: "critical" (the drawing fails to show its scene or is badly broken), "major" (clearly visible, hurts the result), or "minor".
- what: what is wrong, in plain visual language.
- where: which part of the drawing, in words ("the rear wheel", "upper left", "the character's legs").
- expected: what should be true instead, described as an outcome for this drawing's own scene, not as a construction recipe.

Forbidden in defects (they would leak one drawing into the other):
- coordinates, pixel or unit sizes, angles, or any other numbers describing geometry;
- exact colour values (hex, rgb, hsl). Plain colour words like "orange" are fine;
- SVG, XML or any code, element or attribute names;
- any mention of the other image ("unlike A", "as in the other drawing", "like image B");
- anything about the other drawing's layout, pose, positions, colours, style or extra details. Those are free.

Both drawings come from the same request, so they should show the same scene: the same subjects doing the same things. If this drawing is missing a subject or an action that the other drawing clearly shows, list it as a defect of at least "major" severity, described as an outcome for this drawing, never by mentioning the other image.

## Parity

Parity means the two images are of equal quality. Set "parity" to true only if all three hold:
1. on every criterion, the two images' scores differ by at most 1;
2. the two images' total scores (sum of all criteria) differ by at most 1. Several criteria may differ, as long as the totals stay within 1;
3. neither image has a "critical" defect.
Otherwise set it to false. Apply this rule to your own scores exactly; do not override it with an overall impression.
